## Part 0 — Universal habits (apply to every generated trig solution)

Break one of these and the working reads as "not my teacher's" even when the maths is
right.

### 0.1 The split line above the angle

Every reduction is written **twice**: the split appears in a **coloured note above the
angle**, then the reduced form on the next line.

```
        180+30
   sin 210°
   = −sin 30°
```

`TRIG p08, p13, p37–p43`. A solution that jumps straight to `−sin30°` has hidden the step
her kids are trained to write.

### 0.2 Decimal comma, two decimals at the end

`66,42°` · `113,58°` · `−0,7` · `92,43°` (`p46`, `p49`, `p68`). She keeps **more decimals
mid-working** and rounds once at the end: `ref. ∠ = 75,9637°` (`p50`), `25,1487…` →
`x = 92,43°` (`p68`). Never round the reference angle before dividing through.

### 0.3 Roman-numeral quadrants + the tick cross

Quadrants are `I`, `II`, `III`, `IV` in Roman numerals when they head a general-solution
line, and `①②③④` in the wheel diagrams. Every general solution and every special sum
carries a small **cross** with **ticks** in the chosen quadrants (`p27–p30`, `p45–p68`).
This cross is the object the app's round-12 "click a corner" input is copying.

### 0.4 `k∈ℤ` closes the answer, once

Every general solution ends `+ K.360°` (or `+ K.180°`) and the **final** line carries
`, k∈ℤ`. She writes a **capital K** in the term and a lower-case `k` in `k∈ℤ`
(`p45`, `p49`, `p58`). One `k∈ℤ` per question, at the bottom of the last branch.

### 0.5 "no solution"

When a factor demands `sinx = 2`, `sinθ = 3`, `sinθ = −2`, `cosθ = −3/2` or `cosθ = 4/3`,
she writes **`∴ no solution`** (`p54`, `p55`, `p56`, `p57`, `p58`, `p65`). On `p54` she
draws the sine graph with the line `y = −2` never touched, and a `?`. Same word as her
algebra ruling — never "undefined", never "no real solution", never "invalid".

### 0.6 Reference angle comes from the POSITIVE value

`TRIG p44 ①`: *"(don't type − into calculator)"*. The reference angle is always taken from
the size of the number, never from the calculator's negative inverse. `sinθ = −0,256` →
ref ∠ from `0,256`; the sign is handled by choosing the quadrants.

### 0.7 `det K = …`

Her shorthand for "let K = …" is written **`det`** (`p58`, `p65`, `p67`). Read it as "let";
generated text should say **let** in words. The substitution letter is a **capital K**.

### 0.8 `(pyth)` as the reason

Every Pythagoras line ends with `(pyth)` in brackets (`p26`, `p28`, `p29`, `p30`, `p36`).

### 0.9 `∴` and `or`

`∴` opens the conclusion line. Two branches of a factorised equation sit **side by side
separated by `or`** (`p53`–`p59`, `p65`–`p67`), each carrying its own quadrant cross, its
own ref ∠, and its own quadrant lines.

### 0.10 Rationalising

Special-angle values are left **unrationalised**: `tan30° = 1/√3`, `sin45° = 1/√2`
(`p06`, `p41`). But a **special-sums** answer is rationalised: `−2/√5 = −2√5/5` (`p31`).
See flag F12 — this is the one place worth one line from her.

## PART A — Introduction: what trigonometry IS (`p02–p04`) · Round 1

### A1. The word (`p02`)

She breaks the word into three coloured pieces and glosses each:

> **tri**gonometry → **three** · trigo**no**metry → **angled** · trigono**metry** →
> **measurement**
>
> `= the study of angles and of the angular relationships of triangles`

### A2. RATIOS (`p02`)

Boxed: **"can only be used with right triangles"**, with a right-triangle icon.

| Her label | Her definition |
|---|---|
| **Opposite** | *"side that is **across from** the angle θ"* |
| **Adjacent** | *"side that is **next to** the angle θ"* |
| **Hypotenuse** | *"side that is **across from** the right angle"* |

She then draws the **same triangle twice, mirrored**, with O/A/H moved — the point being
that the labels follow θ, not the picture.

### A3. SOHCAHTOA (`p03`)

Three colour-coded cards, in this order:

| Card | Words | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| **SOH** (blue) | Sine · Opposite · Hypotenuse | `sinθ = O/H` |
| **CAH** (purple) | Cosine · Adjacent · Hypotenuse | `cosθ = A/H` |
| **TOA** (green) | Tangent · Opposite · Adjacent | `tanθ = O/A` |

### A4. Where the ratios come from (`p03`) — the discovery story

Under the heading *"where do these ratios originate from"* she draws a circle with a point
on it, radius `r`, and drops `x` and `y` to make a right triangle:

> *"as **point** moves along the circle, the **radius stay the same** BUT*
> - *size of θ*
> - *x-coordinate*
> - *y-coordinates*
>
> *changes in ratio"*

**This is the round-1 discovery beat.** The ratios are not new objects; they are what
happens to `x` and `y` while `r` refuses to move.

### A5. What each ratio MEANS (`p04`) — a three-row table

She gives each function a meaning, a formula, a picture pair (small angle vs large angle
on a quarter circle, radius fixed), and a one-line consequence.

| Function | Her gloss | Formula | What it measures | Small angle → | Large angle → |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **sinθ** | *means "curve"* | `sinθ = y/r` | *"How the **y-coordinate** changes when the angle gets bigger or smaller"* | shorter side | larger side |
| **cosθ** | *"compliment of sinθ"* (her spelling) | `cosθ = x/r` | *"How the **x-coordinate** changes when the angle gets bigger or smaller"* | larger side | shorter side |
| **tanθ** | *"ordinates from the word 'tangent'"* (her spelling) | `tanθ = y/x` , and `= sinθ/cosθ` | *"The relationship as how the x- and y-coordinates **simulateously** change as the angle changes"* | — | — |

Her closing line for tan: *"as the **x-value** gets bigger, the **y-value** gets smaller
and vice versa"*.

Each row is stamped **"radius stays the same"** in a pink highlight. That phrase is the
thread of the whole introduction.

> **Round-1 takeaway in her frame:** a ratio on its own means nothing — it is only "how
> far round" the point has gone. Fix the radius, move the point, watch x and y.

### C6. Identities (`p06`, `p24`, `p32`)

```
tanθ = sinθ/cosθ          1/tanθ = cosθ/sinθ        (p24)

### C7. Worked special-angle sums (`p39–p43`)

The shape of every one: reduce → block-bracket any square → read values off O-A-H → tidy.

```

   tan 240° · cos(−210°) − sin² 315°

   = tan60 · cos150 − [−sin45]²          [180−30 above cos150]
   = tan60 · (−cos30) − (+sin²45)

```

`p43 eg.12`
```
   tan225 + cos300 − sin²510
   = tan45 + cos60 − sin²150
   = tan45 + cos60 − sin²30

## PART D — CO-FUNCTIONS (`p17–p22`) · Round 4 — teach this BEFORE hard reductions

### D1. Why they exist (`p17`) — the two-labelling triangle

She draws the **same 30-60-90 triangle twice**. Left copy: θ at the bottom-right, so
`o = √3`, `a = 1`, `h = 2`. Right copy: the angle at the top, annotated
`180 − 90 − θ = 90 − θ`, so the o and a labels **swap**.

```
   sinθ = √3/2                cos(90 − θ) = √3/2
   cosθ = 1/2                 sin(90 − θ) = 1/2
```

That is the whole argument: **the two acute angles of a right triangle share the sides,
they just call them different names.**

### D2. Co-functions live INSIDE the ASTC wheel

On `p17` and again on `p23`, the wheel gains two extra entries:

| Quadrant | Positive forms | Negative forms | **Co-function form** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** ① | `θ` / `360° + θ` | `θ − 360°` | **`90° − θ`** |
| **S** ② | `180° − θ` | `−180° − θ` | **`90° + θ`** |
| **T** ③ | `180° + θ` | `θ − 180°` | — |
| **C** ④ | `360° − θ` | `−θ` / `−360° − θ` | — |

So `90° − θ` is an **A** angle (everything positive) and `90° + θ` is an **S** angle (only
sine positive). That single fact generates the trap.

### D3. ⚠️ THE TRAP (`p17`, `p24 ④`)

```
   * trap  →   cos(90 + θ) = −sinθ
```

Drawn beside it: a mini cross with `90°` on top, an arrow left to `90+θ` labelled **S**, an
arrow right to `90−θ` labelled **A**.

> **Her framing:** `90 − θ` is friendly (quadrant A, everything positive). `90 + θ` has
> crossed into **S**, so anything that is not sine picks up a minus. Cosine is not sine —
> so `cos(90+θ)` is negative, and it turns into `−sinθ`.

This is the single most-named trap in the whole file. It appears on `p17`, `p18 eg.1`,
`p19 eg.3`, `p19 eg.4`, `p20 eg.5`, `p22 eg.4` and `p24`.

### D4. Negative co-functions (`p21–p22`, `p24 ⑤`)

```
   sin(θ − 90) = −cosθ
   cos(θ − 90) =  sinθ
```

**"but why?"** (`p24`, circled in pink) — her derivation, using the `let K` trick:

```
   sin(θ − 90)                       cos(θ − 90)
   = sin[−(90 − θ)]                  = cos[−(90 − θ)]
              let K = 90 − θ
   = sin(−K)         IV quadrant     = cos(−K)
   = −sin K                          = cos K
   = −sin(90 − θ)                    = cos(90 − θ)
   = −cosθ                           = sinθ
```

The pivot is `−K` sitting in the **IV** quadrant, where cos survives and sin flips.

`p22 eg.3` shows the same result two ways, side by side, with a vertical rule:

```
             +360
   sin(−θ − 90)                  |  or Hayley's way
   = sin(270 − θ)                |     [a cross: 90+θ above, −90−θ below,
   = sin(180 + 90 − θ)           |      marked "x−1", i.e. the same arm
   = −sin(90 − θ)                |      reached by turning the other way]
   = −cosθ                       |  = −cosθ
```

**"Hayley's way"** is a learner's short-cut she liked enough to write down and keep. Use
the name — it is a small, real piece of her classroom.

### D5. Worked co-function shapes (`p18–p20`)

```
p18 eg.1   sin(90−θ)·sinθ            cosθ · sinθ
           ───────────────    =    ──────────────    =  −1
           cosθ·cos(90+θ)          cosθ · (−sinθ)

p20 eg.6   sin(360−θ)·sin(90+θ)       (−sinθ)·cosθ
           ────────────────────  =   ─────────────   =  −1
           cos(90−θ)·cos(360−θ)        sinθ·cosθ
```

### D6. Round-4 input shape (her design)

Steps she wants: **pick the sign (+ / −) → pick cos or sin → type the value on the number
pad.** For a variable question the number-pad step falls away. Mix numerical values and
variables in the same round.

## PART E — REDUCTIONS, numerical values (`p07–p09`, `p13`) · Round 5

### E1. What a reduction IS (`p07`)

> **REDUCTIONS = rewriting angles as acute angles.**

She proves it on the sine graph rather than asserting it:

```
   sin30  = 1/2
   sin150 = sin(180 − 30) = 1/2          ← same height on the curve
   sin210 = sin(180 + 30) = −1/2
   sin330 = sin(360 − 30) = −1/2
```

with the bands marked:

| Band | Her wording |
|---|---|
| 1st quadrant | `0° < x < 90°` |
| 2nd quadrant | `90° < x < 180°` |
| 3rd quadrant | `180° < x < 270°` |
| "4rd" quadrant *(sic — see F1)* | `270° < x < 360°` |

and the closing line: *"repeats every 360°"*, *"all angles can be reduced to acute angles =
have the same y-values"*.

### E2. Her three steps (`p08`)

> ① determine **quadrant**
> ② **reduction formula**
> ③ **`+` or `−` sign**

(The app's round-5 fourth step — "does the ratio stay sin/cos/tan or is it a co-function"
— is her addition for the drill; the notes fold it into ②.)

### E3. Positive angles — the wheel (`p08`)

```
        S: 180° − θ        |        A: θ  /  360° + θ
   ─────────────────────── + ───────────────────────

## PART I — IDENTITIES (`p32–p35`) · Round 9

### I1. The heading and the pro-tips (`p32`)

> **IDENTITIES — prove that**
> ↳ **Show that LHS = RHS**
> ↳ **pro-tips**  * **when in doubt, go LEFT**
> - **identities** — `tanθ = sinθ/cosθ` · `sin²θ + cos²θ = 1` ↳ **masked identities**
> - **factorize (common factor)**
> - **fractions = find LCD**
> - **multiply products**

She works the **LHS only**, top to bottom, and lands on `= RHS`. She never squeezes both
sides toward the middle.

### I2. The four moves, one worked example each

**multiply products** — `p32 eg.1`
```
   (sinθ + cosθ)² = 1 + 2sinθ·cosθ
   LHS = (sinθ + cosθ)²          ← she writes (y + x)² beside it,
       = sin²θ + 2sinθ·cosθ + cos²θ    with a "×2" arrow on the middle term
       = 1 + 2sinθ·cosθ
       = RHS
```

**KFC-style division** — `p33 eg.2`
```
   1/tanθ = cosθ/sinθ
   LHS = 1 ÷ sinθ/cosθ = 1 × cosθ/sinθ = cosθ/sinθ = RHS
```

**co-function inside an identity** — `p33 eg.3`
```
   tan(90 − θ) = 1/tanθ
   LHS = sin(90−θ)/cos(90−θ) = cosθ/sinθ = 1/tanθ = RHS
```

**LCD** — `p34 eg.4`
```
        1            1              2
   ──────────  +  ─────────  =  ────────
    1 − cosθ       1 + cosθ       sin²θ

   LHS = [(1 + cosθ) + (1 − cosθ)] / [(1 + cosθ)(1 − cosθ)]
       = 2 / (1 − cos²θ)
       = 2 / sin²θ                      ← masked identity
       = RHS
```
(she writes the multiplier under each fraction: `×(1+cosθ)` and `×(1−cosθ)`)

**LCD with a `1` on top** — `p35 eg.5`
```
   1/cos²θ − 1 = tan²θ
   LHS = 1/cos²θ − 1/1        ← the 1 gets a denominator, then ×cos²θ
       = (1 − cos²θ)/cos²θ
       = sin²θ/cos²θ
       = tan²θ = RHS
```

**LCD + common factor** — `p35 eg.6` (the one the app's round 9 uses)
```
   tanθ + cosθ/(1 + sinθ) = 1/cosθ
   LHS = sinθ/cosθ + cosθ/(1 + sinθ)
       = [sinθ(1 + sinθ) + cos²θ] / [cosθ(1 + sinθ)]
       = [sinθ + sin²θ + cos²θ] / [cosθ(1 + sinθ)]
       = (sinθ + 1) / [cosθ(sinθ + 1)]
       = 1/cosθ = RHS
```

p55 eg.2   9sinθ·cosθ + 6cosθ = 12sinθ + 8

     III: θ = 180 + 41,81 + K.360° = 221,81° + K.360°
     IV : θ = 360 − 41,81 + K.360° = 318,19° + K.360°,  k∈ℤ
```

### K7. Type ⑤ — *trinomial* (`p56–p59`)

> *"three terms"* · *"one function is squared and the other is not = `sin²θ` and `sinθ`"*

**Plain K-method** (`p56`, `p57`):
```
p56 eg.1   2sin²x + 5sinx − 3 = 0        let K = sinx
           2K² + 5K − 3 = 0
           (2K − 1)(K + 3) = 0
   ∴ K = 1/2 → sinx = 1/2                or   K = −3 → sinx = −3
     ref. ∠ = 30°                              ∴ no solution
     I : x = 30° + K.360°
     II: x = 180 − 30 + K.360° = 150° + K.360°,  k∈ℤ
```

**With a masked identity** (`p58`) — her cue is written out in full:
> *"middle term differs = **rewrite the squared function by means of a masked identity**"*
> boxed: `cos²α = 1 − sin²α`

```
p58 eg.3   2cos²α + 7sinα − 5 = 0
           2(1 − sin²α) + 7sinα − 5 = 0
           2 − 2sin²α + 7sinα − 5 = 0        let K = sinα
           2 − 2K² + 7K − 5 = 0
           0 = 2K² − 7K + 3
           0 = (K − 3)(2K − 1)
   ∴ K = 3 → sinα = 3                  or   K = 1/2 → sinα = 1/2
     ∴ no solution                          ref. ∠ = 30°
                                            I : α = 30° + K.360°
                                            II: α = 150° + K.360°,  k∈ℤ
```

**Masked identity without a K** (`p59` — she skips the substitution when the trinomial
collapses to a common factor):
```
p59 eg.4   1 + sinθ = cos²θ
           0 = cos²θ − sinθ − 1
           0 = 1 − sin²θ − sinθ − 1
           sin²θ + sinθ = 0
           sinθ(sinθ + 1) = 0
   ∴ sinθ = 0                      or   sinθ = −1
     ref. ∠ = 0                          ref. ∠ = 90°
     I : θ = 0° + K.360°                 III: θ = 180 + 90 + K.360° = 270° + K.360°,  k∈ℤ
     II: θ = 180° + K.360°
```

Note `sinθ = −1` → **`ref. ∠ = 90°` and quadrant III**. She runs the same machine even at a
boundary value rather than reading `270°` off the graph. Keep that — it is the habit that
makes the routine reliable for her kids.

### K8. Type ⑥ — *co-functions* (`p60–p61`)

> *"sin and cos with **different angles**"*

Rule: **make both sides the same function** using the `90 −` co-function, then match the
two brackets. `sin = sin` uses **I and II**; `cos = cos` uses **I and IV**.

```
p60 eg.1   sin2θ = cos57            [90 − 33]
           sin2θ = sin33            ✓|✓  (top: I and II)
   I : 2θ/2 = 33/2 + K.360°/2   →  θ = 16,5° + K.180°
   II: 2θ = 180 − 33 + K.360°   →  2θ = 147 + K.360°  →  θ = 73,5° + K.180°,  k∈ℤ
```

```
p60 eg.2   cos(3θ + 10) = sinθ
           cos(3θ + 10) = cos(90 − θ)      ✓ right (I and IV)
                LHS            RHS
   I : 3θ + 10 = 90 − θ + K.360°
       3θ + θ  = 90 − 10 + K.360°
       4θ = 80 + K.360°   →  θ = 20° + K.90°
   IV: 3θ + 10 = 360 − (90 − θ) + K.360°
       3θ + 10 = 360 − 90 + θ + K.360°
       3θ − θ  = 270 − 10 + K.360°
       2θ = 260 + K.360°  →  θ = 130° + K.180°,  k∈ℤ
```

```
p61 eg.3   sin(θ − 50) = cos2θ
           sin(θ − 50) = sin(90 − 2θ)      ✓|✓ (top)
   I : θ − 50 = 90 − 2θ + K.360°   →  3θ = 140 + K.360°  →  θ = 46,67° + K.120°
   II: θ − 50 = 180 − (90 − 2θ) + K.360°
       θ − 50 = 180 − 90 + 2θ + K.360°
       θ − 2θ = 90 + 50 + K.360°
       −θ = 140° + K.360°   →  θ = −140° + K.360°,  k∈ℤ
```

### L2. The reference angle

Written as **`ref. ∠ = …`** on its own line, always **positive**, taken from the size of

`ref. ∠ = 0` for `sinθ = 0` / `cosθ = ±1`; `ref. ∠ = 90°` for `sinθ = ±1`.

## PART M — UNDEFINED VALUES (`p62–p63`) · Round 13

> **UNDEFINED VALUES** ↳ **denominator = 0**

Her routine: **list every denominator, set each = 0, solve each as a general solution, then
read the interval off the list (or off a graph).**

**`p62 eg.1`** — Given `tanx + cosx/(1 + sinx) = 1/cosx`. For which values of `x` is the
identity undefined for the interval `x ∈ (−180°;180°)`?

```
   [boxed in red]   1 + sinx = 0        or        cosx = 0
                    sinx = −1                     ref. ∠ = 90°
                    ref. ∠ = 90°   ✓|✓ bottom        ✓|✓ right
   III: x = 270° + K.360°           I : x = 90° + K.360°

   ∴  x ∈ {−90°; 90°}
```
with a graph of `y = 1 + sinx` and `y = cosx` below, the two crossings at `−90` and `90`
**circled in red**.

Note that `tanx` contributes `cosx = 0` — she does not write `tanx` separately; the `cosx`
condition covers it.

**`p63 eg.2`** — `1/(1−sinx) − 1/(1+sinx) = 2tanx/cosx`. For which values of `x` is the
identity undefined?

```
   1 − sinx = 0      or      1 + sinx = 0      or      cosx = 0
```
Then a single graph of all three curves, with the zeros circled at `−270, −90, 90, 270` and
a green arrow marked **`180°`** between two of them — the spacing *is* the answer:

```
   x = 90° + K.180°,  k∈ℤ
```

**The teaching beat:** three separate conditions collapse into **one** family because the
zeros sit 180° apart. She shows it on the picture rather than proving it.

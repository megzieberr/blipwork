These are the rounds she wants, in order, with the page refs she gave. Build them via
the add-chapter skill when their turn comes (digest first — her ruling).

1. **Introduction round — discovery only.** What trigonometry stands for; where the
   ratios come from (a point rotating around a circle); the meaning of sine, cosine and
   tan; how they are just ratios that mean nothing without the angle. (pp. 1–4)
2. **Discovery round — Cartesian plane.** Show the sine, cosine and tan graphs and where
   each is above or below the x-axis; connect it to the story **All Strippers Take
   Cash**; show why each ratio is positive or negative in each quadrant. (p. 5)
3. **Discovery round — special angles & identities.** First the special triangles: how
   90-45-45 comes from a square and 90-60-30 from an equilateral triangle. Then the
   story **Oats Are Healthy** — the table builds in THIS order: draw the table; O A H
   appears in the left column; then 30, 45, 60 appear in the top row; then the values
   appear left to right: `1 1 √3` / `√3 1 1` / `2 √2 2`. One or two slides of questions
   where they pick the correct value. Then the tan identity and the masked identities.
   (p. 6)
4. **Co-functions — do this round FIRST** (before reductions). A bunch of known values
   where the kids convert between sine and cosine. They must learn the trap
   `cos(90° + x) = −sin x`. Mix numerical values and variables already in this round.
   Steps: pick the sign (+ or −) → pick cos or sin → type the value on the number pad.
   For a variable question the number-pad step just falls away. (p. 17)
5. **Reductions — numerical values.** One round drilling numbers, mixing positive,
   negative and co-functions immediately. Four steps: (1) the quadrant (pick 1–4);
   (2) the reduction formula (number pad — they type `180−`, `180+`, etc.); (3) the
   sign (+ or −); (4) the trig ratio (does it stay sin/cos/tan, or is it a co-function
   where it changes). (pp. 7–22)
6. **Reductions Tip Chips.** Just revises the tips on pp. 23–25.
7. **Reductions — unknown variables.** They pick two things: the sign and the trig
   ratio (the value is just x or θ).
8. **Cartesian plane — special sums.** Give the ratio, e.g. `tan x = 2/5` with x between
   90° and 270°. They pick the quadrant the triangle lies in; the app draws the
   triangle; they type the value of each side (number pad); then the app asks the
   questions and they pick the value from a list (no number pad — it's fractions now).
   (pp. 26–31)
9. **Identities — no full worked questions.** They only identify identities and next
   steps: given a question, pick from a list which part we work with next and what to
   do with it. E.g. `1/(1+cos x) + 1/(1−cos x) = 2/sin x` → pick LCD and what the LCD
   is; `(sin x + sin²x + cos²x)/cos x` → pick `sin²x + cos²x` and type `1`; a masked
   identity → pick which masked identity it is. (pp. 32–35)
10. **Super special sums — triangle sides ONLY.** The app gives the triangle in the
    first quadrant; they pick where the 1 and the k (or t) go, then pick from a list:
    `√(1 − t²)` or `√(t² − 1)`. (pp. 36–38)
11. **General solution — the 6 types.** The app gives a question; they click ONE of six
    options. Give the number AND the name: type 1: function alone; type 2: same angles;
    type 3: common factor; etc. (pp. 44–61)
12. **General solution — last steps.** (1) which quadrant and (2) the reference angle
    (number pad). For `sin x = 1/2` they pick quadrants 1 and 2 — and the cute bit: no
    list 1–4, but a CROSS (a vertical and a horizontal line), and they click in the
    top-right corner to put a check mark in quadrant 1, bottom-left for quadrant 3.
    Plus a button **"no reference angle"** for co-function questions like
    `sin(x + 80°) = sin 2x`.
13. **Undefined values.** Given an equation, e.g. `tan x + cos x/(1 + sin x) = 1/cos x`,
    they pick `1 + sin x` and `cos x` from a list and type on the number pad what those
    must be equated to. (pp. 62–64)
